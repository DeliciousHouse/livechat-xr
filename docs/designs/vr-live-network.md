# Design: VR Live network: cross-platform discovery for small VR streamers

Generated by /office-hours on 2026-10-16
Branch: main
Repo: DeliciousHouse/livechat-xr
Status: APPROVED
Mode: Startup

## Problem Statement

Small VR streamers (1-10 viewers) can't get found. They multistream the same session to TikTok, Twitch and YouTube, and every existing discovery tool is single-platform and built for bigger channels. LiveChat XR's original pitch, chat inside the headset, is being covered by BeamXR and Meta (roadmap Gate 1 failed on the BeamXR verdict "they will ship this soon - partner or get out"), so the product needs a problem streamers actually feel.

## Demand Evidence

- Founder's own TikTok streams, Oct 10-16 (`%LOCALAPPDATA%\LiveChatXR\sessions.jsonl`): about 40 hours streamed, peak 1-4 viewers per stream, 5 new follows (about 0.13 per hour). The follow-ask existed but was switched off, so 0 were shown.
- Hosted relay, 35 sessions across 5 other streamers: 7 new follows in total.
- When shown those numbers, the founder picked "Getting found" as the growth pain, over converting or engaging existing viewers. No other streamer has been asked yet.
- The VR live census (`hermes-work/vr-census-tool/census.db`, Oct 11-16): 3,370 distinct VR streamers seen live on four games; 470 seen on 3 or more days (Twitch 244, YouTube 151, TikTok 75, with TikTok undercounted because its request budget and a $5 Apify cap ran out). Population: ONE alone: 325 streamers, 39 regulars.
- Willingness to pay is **unproven**. Desire for growth is real; nobody has offered money for discovery.

## Status Quo

- Chat while playing: the founder peeks at a phone or monitor. Pop (`@1poplightningvr`) puts an earbud in one ear and has a separate app read chat aloud. Both workarounds cost focus mid-match.
- Discovery: streamers rely on short clips (the founder runs his own Pop1 clip pipeline), raids among friends, and single-platform tools. Twitch's raid browser covers Twitch only; Streams Charts says its raid finder does not cover small creators; Twitch Roulette-style finders are Twitch-only.

## Target User & Narrowest Wedge

- Target: a VR streamer with 1-10 viewers who multistreams, starting with Population: ONE (the founder, Pop, Timika, Charlie, Naiya and Lola already use the relay).
- Narrowest wedge: a "who's live in Population: ONE right now" list across all three platforms, ordered so the smallest streams surface first, so small streamers find raid and collab partners and viewers find them.

## Constraints

- The census runs on capped budgets: TikTok's daily request budget is used up, and the Apify actor is at its $5 monthly cap. Coverage of TikTok is incomplete.
- YouTube Data API live search costs 100 quota units per call against a 10,000-unit daily default, so about 100 live searches a day.
- A public directory built from platform data raises terms-of-service questions; official APIs exist for Twitch (Helix) and YouTube (Data API with quota), not for TikTok LIVE discovery.
- The founder's clip pipeline is his own and not a product for others.
- The chat relay stays free with donations.

## Premises

1. LiveChat XR's edge is **cross-platform live presence**: knowing which small VR streamers are live right now on TikTok, Twitch and YouTube together. The merged chat relay is the free on-ramp that makes this possible, because the relay knows in real time when an opted-in streamer is live on TikTok, the platform the census covers worst. No competitor covers all three (Restream cannot read TikTok chat; BeamXR documents Twitch and YouTube).
2. **Hypothesis, not fact:** getting found is the growth pain small VR streamers feel most, and some will pay to fix it. Only the founder has said so. The assignment asks the five relay streamers first, and Stage 0 tests whether a live list produces discovery; the paid offer starts in Stage 0.
3. The assets are the census of live VR streamers and the relay's real-time view of opted-in streamers. The clip pipeline is the founder's own.
4. The chat relay stays free with donations. Anything paid must show results in follows or raids within a week or two.

## Cross-Model Perspective

An independent Codex cold read (gpt-6-astra) steelmanned the product as helping small VR creators turn gameplay into new followers across all three platforms, with free merged chat feeding moment signals and a paid service delivering clips and measuring follower gains. Its key point: "Getting found" is the most revealing answer, because it moves the goal from reading existing viewers to reaching new ones. It rejected the premise that streamers would pay for discovery (Critical): desire is not willingness to pay. It recommended a 48-hour concierge clip test with a $10 offer instead of building a discovery platform first. Premise 2 was revised into a hypothesis with the payment ask starting in Stage 0; the founder chose the discovery network over the clip test.

## Approaches Considered

- **A: Clip-moment recap.** After each stream, post 3 moments worth clipping to Discord from chat bursts, gifts, follows and a live `!clip` command. Small build on the relay. Not chosen: it helps streamers clip, but the founder wants to solve discovery directly.
- **B: VR Live network (chosen).** A cross-platform "who's live in VR right now" list and Discord bot built from the census and the relay.
- **C: Concierge clip test.** Hand-make clips for the five relay streamers and offer more for $10. Not chosen: services work that doesn't scale and competes with the founder's time.

## Recommended Approach

**B: VR Live network**, staged so demand and willingness to pay are proven before anything is built for scale. Population: ONE only until a stage says otherwise.

### Free versus paid

> **Design review 2026-10-17 (DR1B, DR1B-gates):** no paid tier at launch. Everything below about Spotlight pricing, pinning, checkout and refunds is on hold until the payment trigger in DR1B-gates fires; until then the list is strictly fewest viewers first and the go-live DM is free for every opted-in streamer.

- **Free listing:** every opted-in streamer appears in the live list, ordered by fewest viewers first, with platform and link.
- **Paid "Spotlight", $3 per month:** the streamer is pinned at the top of the list while live during the 6-11pm PT update window (the offer says so), gets a Discord DM at any hour when another opted-in Pop1 streamer of similar size goes live, and goes first in viewer game-follow pings and in squad-up matches (D7). Squad-up and viewer pings stay free for everyone else; Spotlight buys going first, and a Spotlight streamer stays in pings and squad matches even above 10 viewers (D13).
- **Multistreamers (D17):** each listing appears once, with a link for every platform it is live on; its size for "small" and matching is its highest single-platform viewer count; each platform link has its own shortener so clicks show which platform viewers pick. A listing never matches itself.
- **Similar size, one rule everywhere (D13):** two streams are similar in size if either one's range of half to double its viewers includes the other's count; streams at 0-2 viewers count as covering 0-4. The Spotlight DM and squad-up both use this rule, so matches are always mutual. Spotlight sells only the go-live alert; no platform reports when a stream is about to end. Nothing in the free tier includes pinning, priority or the similar-size DM.
- **Payment:** a Stripe payment link for Spotlight, using the relay's existing Stripe account, webhook endpoint and billing portal. The current webhook only upgrades relay accounts (`server/server.py:1034`), so Spotlight needs a new handler branch and a listing record type: `client_reference_id` carries the listing ID, and the new branch marks Spotlight on that listing. Cancelling through the existing billing portal ends Spotlight at the end of the paid month; a refund is given on request within 7 days.
- **Payment details (D12):** Stage 0 uses a separate one-time $3 Spotlight payment link whose checkouts the relay's webhook ignores; the founder records and reconciles those payers by hand. When Stage 1 launches, each Stage 0 payer opts in, links Discord and passes review, then the founder sets Spotlight on their listing for one month (D13). From Stage 1 the new webhook branch stores the Stripe customer and subscription IDs on the listing when checkout completes, and matches later subscription updated and deleted events by those IDs, so Spotlight ends when a subscription lapses or is cancelled. At checkout it also checks that the listing is reviewed and doesn't already have Spotlight, and refunds automatically otherwise (D13). Streamers without a relay account manage billing through the Stripe customer portal's login link. Checkout is offered only to reviewed listings; a payment from a listing later rejected (for example, as a minor) is refunded in full.
- **What counts as a commitment:** paying the first $3 month in advance through the payment link. A reply saying "I'd pay" does not count. Commitments made before Stage 1 exists are refunded if Stage 1 never launches.

### Who is listed, and safety

- **Stage 0 (hand-posted, one community Discord):** the founder copies the list by hand at post time from the public Population: ONE pages on Twitch (the category directory) and YouTube (a live search for the game title, which misses streams that don't name the game), plus opted-in relay streamers on TikTok. No API output is posted in Stage 0. Every post ends with "reply or DM to be removed".
- **Stage 1 onward (bot and any page):** **opt-in only.** A streamer must opt in to be listed at all. Reasoning: an automated, persistent public aggregate of 1-10-viewer streams is new exposure the platforms don't provide, and Population: ONE has young players.
- **Minors:** never list a channel whose title, bio or stream states or suggests the streamer is under 18. When unsure, leave them out. In Stage 0 the founder checks this while copying each entry. From Stage 1, every new opt-in is held until the founder or relay-ops reviews the channel for age signals, within 24 hours, and any report triggers a re-review before the next update.
- **Platform terms:** before Stage 1 ships, read the Twitch Developer Agreement and the YouTube API Services Terms and Developer Policies for display, caching and retention rules, and keep only current live status plus handle, game, platform, viewer count and link in the public list. TikTok entries come only from opted-in relay streamers, never from scraping, in anything public.

### Opt-in, opt-out and removal

- **Opt in:** Discord `/optin <platform> <handle>` accepts Twitch and YouTube only. The bot gives a short code; the streamer puts it in their stream title or channel bio for one check through the official API, then removes it. TikTok streamers opt in only from their relay manage page, and their listing stays hidden until they prove channel ownership by typing a one-time code in their own TikTok LIVE chat, which the relay sees from the owner account (D18). The relay's sign-up check only confirms that a handle exists, so it is not ownership proof. In-game name and timezone come from `/profile ign:<name> tz:<IANA zone>` or the matching manage-page fields (D12); squad-up and the best-time hint stay off for a listing until both are set.
- **DM check (D15):** at opt-in the bot sends a welcome DM. Spotlight checkout, the similar-size DM, squad-up and the best-time hint stay off for that listing until the welcome DM succeeds, and the bot explains how to allow DMs from server members. Any later DM failure is recorded on the listing and shown on the streamer's manage page and the relay admin page.
- **Opt out:** `/optout`, a reply or DM, or unticking the manage-page box. Removal takes effect within 10 minutes everywhere, including lists already posted.
- **Already-posted lists:** Stage 0 posts are deleted the morning after each evening, and on a removal request the founder edits every earlier post that names that streamer the same day. From Stage 1 the bot keeps a single live-list message that it edits in place, so a removed streamer disappears from it at the next edit; `/live` answers are ephemeral (visible only to the person who asked) and never stay in the channel.
- **One listings database** (a SQLite `listings` table next to `census.db` on Dora, written in transactions because the bot and the relay server both write it; D12) holds, per listing: platform handles, verified Discord user ID, in-game name, review status (pending, reviewed, removed), opt-in source (`/optin` or relay box), Spotlight status, squad flag with its expiry, and timezone. The collectors on Dora, the bot and any page all read it.
- **Discord link before DM features:** relay-box opt-ins have no Discord identity, so the relay manage page gets a "Link Discord" step (OAuth `identify` scope) before squad-up, the best-time DM or Spotlight DMs turn on for that listing.
- **Reviewed listings only:** every feature (list, pings, squad-up, best-time hint) reads only reviewed, active listings. Relay-box opt-ins enter the same 24-hour minors review as `/optin`.

### Data path and freshness

- **Stage 0 runs before the collectors move.** Its list is copied by hand from the public pages at post time, so it is as fresh as the moment it's posted.
- **Collectors move to Dora for Stage 1:** the census collectors run as a cron on Dora with the Twitch and YouTube keys in Dora's env file, writing `census.db` there. The bot reads it read-only. Owner: the relay-ops role.
- **Cadence for one game:** Twitch Helix every 5 minutes (all four tracked games can stay on Twitch, which is cheap); YouTube live search every 15 minutes for Population: ONE only, with the other three games paused on YouTube (96 calls a day, 9,600 of 10,000 units [estimated]). The census already hit YouTube's quota limit (HTTP 429) during its 2026-10-16 validation, so Stage 1 cannot start until a re-measure shows Pop1-only polling stays under quota, or a separate API project or quota increase is in place; TikTok from the relay's own live status for opted-in relay streamers, in real time. The exact TikTok live-presence contract (reconnects, missing viewer counts, how a stream is confirmed as Pop1) is still open: first check whether TikTok room data exposes a game tag (O6, D22 answer C).
- **Freshness rule:** entries seen in the last 15 minutes show normally; 15-30 minutes old show "last seen N min ago"; older than 30 minutes are hidden. The bot re-edits its single live-list message every 5 minutes from 6pm to 11pm US Pacific, so no posted list is ever older than 5 minutes; outside that window the message reads "Updates 6-11pm PT" (D12). `/live` answers and similar-size DMs work at all hours (D13).
- **Adding games** needs more YouTube quota (a quota increase request) or fewer YouTube checks per game. That's a Stage 2 decision.

### Stages and exits

- **Assignment, first:** ask Timika, Pop, Charlie, Naiya and Lola to rank three growth pains: getting found, converting viewers to followers, keeping viewers engaged. If getting found isn't the top pain for at least 3 of the 5, stop and revisit approach A.
- **Stage 0, concierge (no deployed code):** three evenings, posted 30 minutes before peak Pop1 hours (the founder copies the list before going live, or a friend posts it). Each listed streamer gets a link from an existing link shortener with click counts, so no relay code is needed. On evening 3 the founder DMs every listed streamer who is a member of that Discord the question "Would you pay $3/mo to be pinned at the top?" and counts the "I'd pay" answers (DR1B; nothing is sold).
  - **Measured:** shortener clicks; "I went" reactions; raids reported by listed streamers in a two-question form after each evening; public follower counts of listed streamers read by hand from their profiles before evening 1 and after evening 3.
  - **Hand-made squad test (D20):** on evenings 2 and 3 the founder DM-introduces 2-3 pairs of small live streamers who could squad up, and counts the pairs that played together. This gives squad-up evidence before any stop decision.
  - **Launch cohort (D21):** during Stage 0 the founder asks every listed streamer to opt in. Stage 1 starts only after at least 5 opted-in Pop1 streamers are live together at least once between 6 and 11pm PT.
  - **Organic only:** the founder may ask relay friends to raid, but those actions are recorded separately and never count toward the thresholds.
  - **Counting visits (E6, eng D7):** a visit is the shortener's unique-visitor count, never total clicks; the founder tests on separate links that are never posted; a visit bar (5 here, 20 in Stage 1) counts only when its visits come from at least 3 different listed streamers' links.
  - **Pass:** at least 5 organic visits or raids and at least 1 "I'd pay" from a streamer outside the five relay streamers (DR1B-gates). Go to Stage 1.
  - **Middle (1-4 organic actions, or no "I'd pay"):** run three more evenings in a second Pop1 Discord.
  - **After six evenings:** pass only on the pass bar; any other result stops the network, and the founder revisits approach A or C.
- **Stage 1, Discord bot (Pop1 only, opt-in):** keeps the single live-list message updated during peak hours and answers `/live`. Nothing is sold at launch; the welcome DM and `/profile` collect "I'd pay" taps (DR1B).
  - **Measured:** verified opt-ins outside the five relay streamers (streamer count); `/live` calls (viewer-side, reported separately); organic raids and visits via shortener links; "I'd pay" taps.
  - **Advance:** after 2 weeks, at least 10 verified opt-ins outside the five, at least 3 "I'd pay" taps from streamers outside the five, and at least 20 organic shortener visits or 5 organic raids over the 2 weeks (D19, E6, DR1B-gates).
  - **Stop or pivot:** fewer than 3 outside opt-ins after 2 weeks (DR1B-gates dropped the zero-payer clause). Pivot to approach A.
  - **Middle (any other result):** extend two weeks into a second Pop1 Discord, then advance only on the advance bar; any other result pivots to approach A.
- **Stage 1 core and clock (D8, D11):** the 2-week clock starts when the core ships: the live list, `/live`, `/optin` and the free go-live alert (DR1B). The 2-week advance, stop and middle decisions judge the core only. Additions start only after the core's 2-week decision, so "week N" below counts from that decision date (D20, reopening D11). Each addition gets its own 2-week test from the day it ships. If the core stops, unshipped additions are dropped and shipped ones are removed; in the middle band, additions wait until the extension's decision, the advance bar counts opt-ins and payers cumulatively across both Discords, the bot stays in the first Discord, and X4's role-size rule is judged separately in each Discord (D13); on advance, they keep shipping and are judged on their own clocks. Opt-ins are reported by source; relay-box opt-ins count toward the advance bar only after that streamer's first listed live session.
- **Moderator permission gate (D12, D13):** before Stage 0 evening 1, the host Discord's moderators give written permission for the hand-posted lists and the "would you pay" DMs (DR1B); if they won't, Stage 0 runs in the fallback founder-run Discord. Before the core ships in any host Discord, its moderators give written permission for the live list, the follow role, its pings and bot DMs. If the fallback founder-run Discord is used, the viewer-ping cut rule judges clicks per ping only, not role size.
- **Stage 1 additions (approved in /plan-ceo-review, 2026-10-16), one per week after the core's decision, in this order, each with one metric and one cut rule:**
  - **Week 1, relay funnel (X2, D3):** an unticked "list me in Pop1 Live" box on the relay sign-up form and manage page, using the relay's existing TikTok handle check. Metric: relay-box opt-ins that go live on the list. Cut if fewer than 2 do so in 2 weeks.
  - **Week 2, viewer game-follow (X4, D5):** a Discord role viewers join with `/follow pop1`. The bot pings the role at 6:30, 8:00 and 9:30pm US Pacific, only when at least one small opted-in stream is live. Each ping is a role mention plus a link to the single live-list message, which already lists every live small stream, fewest viewers first (D10, D13; no Spotlight section at launch, DR1B). The ping's opening text follows DR6A. A stream leaves the list when it ends, its streamer opts out, or its listing is removed. The bot deletes the ping messages at 11pm. Each entry links through the shortener so visits count as organic. Metric: clicks per ping. Cut if under 1 click per ping on average over 2 weeks, or if role members drop by a third.
  - **Week 3, squad-up (X1, D2):** a live opted-in streamer runs `/squad` to flag "looking for squad"; the flag clears at stream end or after 60 minutes. Matches are other flagged streams of similar size under the one rule above (no paid priority at launch, DR1B). "Invite" is a Discord button that DMs both players each other's in-game name, because Pop1 squads form in the game and no API can send the invite. The bot deletes its squad-offer DMs on both sides when either stream ends, either streamer opts out, or a listing is removed (D12). When it deletes them at stream end, it leaves a DM with no in-game name and a "we played" button for 2 hours. Every squad DM also has "Report" and "Don't match me with them" buttons (D16): either hides the two streamers from each other's matches; a report also queues the reported listing for founder review within 24 hours, and a second report from a different streamer pauses that listing until review. Metric: "we played" taps, at most one counted per pair (D13). Cut if fewer than 3 in 2 weeks.
  - **Best-time hint (X3):** deferred to `TODOS.md` (D23, reopening D4) until viewer-ping clicks by hour can show when viewers actually come.
  - **Shared definition:** "small" means 10 or fewer live viewers everywhere in this plan, matching the 1-10 viewer target user (D12).
  - **Deferred:** post-stream recap (X5, D6) and best-time hint (X3, D23), recorded in `TODOS.md`.
- **Stage 2, public page (Pop1 only):** a read-only page on the relay host, opt-in streamers only, same freshness rule. Built only after Stage 1 advances. Adding a second game needs the quota decision above and at least 5 Stage 1 opt-ins asking for it.

### R2-1 — consistency (blocking)

**Problem**

> Stage 0 is called 'no deployed code' and the Distribution Plan says it 'needs no deployed software', yet Stage 0 depends on per-streamer `/go/<handle>` redirect links on the relay host that count clicks. That is a new route on the relay web server and needs code, storage for click counts, and a pass through the reviewed-main deploy before evening 1. The Stage 0 build and its lead time are hidden.

**Remedy**

> Either list the `/go/<handle>` click-counting route as a small Stage 0 build with its deploy step and a ready-by date before evening 1, or use an existing link shortener with click counts and drop 'on the relay host'. Then make the 'no deployed code' wording match.

### R2-2 — feasibility (blocking)

**Problem**

> Stage 0 posts a 'live now' list from a saved census query, but the committed cadence and freshness rule belong to the collectors after they move to Dora, which is a deployed cron. The document does not say whether Stage 0 runs before or after that move. If before, the query reads the current hourly census with exhausted budgets, so a hand-posted 'live now' list can include streams that already ended, the failure R1-7 was raised about.

**Remedy**

> State whether the Dora collector move happens before Stage 0. If not, say how the Stage 0 list is made fresh at post time (for example, the saved query triggers a one-off Twitch Helix and YouTube pull for Population: ONE right before posting) and apply the same 15/30-minute freshness rule to the post.

### R2-3 — completeness (blocking)

**Problem**

> The Stage 0 pass bar and Success Criteria hinge on a 'Spotlight commitment', but a commitment is never defined. A reply saying 'I'd pay' is the desire-not-payment signal the Cross-Model Perspective warned about; a prepayment or a pledge with payment details is real willingness to pay. The willingness-to-pay test cannot be scored without this.

**Remedy**

> Define what counts as a commitment (for example, paid the first $3 month in advance, or a refundable deposit, not a verbal yes) and how it is recorded.

### R2-4 — feasibility (blocking)

**Problem**

> The Spotlight offer appears only in the community Discord post, but most streamers listed in Stage 0 are Twitch and YouTube streamers pulled from the category who never opted in and may not be members of that Discord. The pass bar needs a commitment from someone outside the five relay streamers, yet nothing ensures the offer reaches those people, so a failed pass may mean the offer was never seen rather than unwanted.

**Remedy**

> Say how the offer reaches listed outside streamers (for example, a direct message or channel reply to each listed streamer with the offer and link), or restrict the measured offer to people in the Discord and count only them.

### R2-5 — completeness (blocking)

**Problem**

> Stage 1 sells Spotlight from day 1 and its advance and stop rules count 'paying Spotlight users', but the document names no payment method, no way to tie a payment to a verified opted-in handle, and no cancellation or refund behavior. The bot cannot sell or verify the product as described.

**Remedy**

> Choose the payment path (for example, a Ko-fi or Stripe payment link, or Discord server subscriptions), state how a payment is matched to the opted-in streamer's record in suppress.json, and what happens when a month lapses.

### R2-6 — feasibility (blocking)

**Problem**

**Remedy**

> Define 'about to end' with a source that exists (for example, the streamer types `/ending` in Discord or ticks a box on the relay manage page), or drop it and sell only the go-live alert.

### R2-7 — clarity (blocking)

**Problem**

**Remedy**

### Design decisions
- **DR1A, live-list message (answer 1A-A, 2026-10-17):** one plain-text message, no embeds, link previews suppressed (SUPPRESS_EMBEDS). Line 1: `**Pop1 Live** · N small streams live · updated <t:UNIX:R>` (Discord's relative timestamp renders on each viewer's device, so a frozen list shows its age even if the bot is down). Rows: `**Name** · 3 viewers · [Twitch](short) · [TikTok](short)` with platforms spelled as words (no custom emoji); entries 15-30 min old append `· last seen 18 min ago`. Footer: `` `/live` anytime · `/follow pop1` for pings · `/optin` to get listed ``. Over 2,000 characters: last row becomes `+N more — /live for all`. `/live` uses the same renderer, ephemeral.
- **DR1B, no paid tier at launch (Brendan: "i want it to be free at first. if there's a demand then i'll ask for payment"; answer A, 2026-10-17):** reopens CEO D7/D12/D13 payment. The list is strictly fewest viewers first with no pinned section; the similar-size go-live DM is free for every opted-in streamer who turns it on; pings and squad-up have no paid priority. Demand check: the welcome DM and `/profile` offer "Would you pay $3/mo to be pinned at the top? [Yes, I'd pay]", one count per streamer. Stripe checkout, the Spotlight webhook branch and its dedupe/refund (eng T15, outside voice #2) wait for the payment trigger.
- **DR1B-gates, demand rules (answer A, 2026-10-17):** Stage 0 passes on at least 5 organic visits or raids plus at least 1 "I'd pay" from a streamer outside the five (founder DM poll). Stage 1 advances on at least 10 outside opt-ins, at least 3 outside "I'd pay" taps, and at least 20 organic visits or 5 organic raids (E6 counting); stop below 3 outside opt-ins (zero-payer clause dropped). Payment trigger: once 3 outside streamers tap "I'd pay", build checkout (T15); if fewer than 2 actually pay within 2 weeks of checkout launch, Spotlight stays free and payment is shelved.
- **DR1C, bot identity (answer 1C-A, 2026-10-17):** display name "Pop1 Live" with the LiveChat XR violet avatar; profile "by LiveChat XR · livechat.deliciouswines.org". Every DM and ping starts with `Pop1 Live:` so a Quest pop-up names its sender.
- **DR1D, manage-page listing setup (answer 1D-A, 2026-10-17):** a "Pop1 Live listing" section, separate from chat-relay controls. Status line first: Not requested / Setting up (step N of 5) / Waiting for review (within 24h) / Listed when you're live / Needs attention / Removed. Then five steps in `{components.step-number}` gold: 1 List me, 2 Link Discord, 3 In-game name + timezone, 4 Prove your channel, 5 Review. Done steps show ✓, only the next step carries the `{components.button-primary}`, later steps use `{colors.text-label}` (per DR6C).

- **DR2A, list states (answer 2A-A, 2026-10-17):** nobody live: `No small Pop1 streams live right now · updated <t:R>. Going live? /optin to be listed.` Outside the window: `The list updates <t:6pm:t>–<t:11pm:t> each night (your time). /live works anytime.` Delayed: header `⚠ Live data delayed since <t:R> — /live may be out of date too`, entries kept until 30 min old. Network off, or the last edit before a planned stop: `Pop1 Live is paused — back soon.`
- **DR2B, per-source staleness (answer 2B-A, 2026-10-17; refines E1):** each collector (Twitch, YouTube) is tracked separately with the same 15-minute threshold and 6-11pm PT window. Header names the source: `⚠ YouTube streams delayed since <t:R> · others current`. Founder DM `Pop1 Live: YouTube collector stale since 8:05pm (HTTP 429). /admin`, recovery DM on return; an incident closes only after 2 good polls in a row. /admin shows one row per source.

A person checks every new listing within 24h — we'll DM you when you're on.` Approved: `Pop1 Live: you're on. - **DR2D, /optin auto-check (Brendan: "wait i meant autocheck", option 2D-B, 2026-10-17):** no Check button. The ephemeral /optin reply shows the code in a code block, valid 15 minutes: `Put this in your Twitch title or bio — I'll check every minute.` The bot rechecks every minute and edits that same reply live (`Checking… not found yet (titles can take a minute)`, `That code is on a different channel than @handle`, `Verified ✓ You can remove the code now.`), so a streamer with blocked DMs still sees the result; the 15-minute limit matches Discord's window for editing an interaction reply. Expired: `Code expired — run /optin again.` After success the welcome DM goes out; if Discord blocks it, the reply adds `I couldn't DM you. - **DR8, platform-terms design changes (Brendan: "ok do the design changes then", 2026-10-17 15:00 PDT; option 1 after the Stage 1 terms review in PR #35, `docs/designs/vr-live-network-stage1-gates.md`).** This decision supersedes DR1A, D17, D13 and DR7B where they conflict. It is a product design meant to stay inside the Twitch Developer Agreement and the YouTube API policies as read in that review; it is not legal sign-off.
  - **Per-platform counts, no blended number:**
    - Each row shows each platform's own count and label: `**Brendan** · Twitch 4 · TikTok 2 · [Watch on Twitch](…) · [Watch on TikTok](…)`.
    - No cross-platform maximum, sum or other combined viewer number is shown, stored or used. This replaces D17's "highest single-platform count".
  - **Sort, "small" and matching use one raw source count:**
    - The listing's **primary count** is the count of its first live platform, in this order: Twitch, then YouTube, then TikTok.
    - It is never derived from several platforms.
    - It drives fewest-first ordering, the ≤10 "small" rule, similar-size matching (D13) and "about your size" (DR7B).
  - **Branding and links:**
    - Each platform link reads `Watch on Twitch` / `Watch on YouTube` / `Watch on TikTok`, so the platform name is the clickable link text.
    - Each link resolves straight to that platform's own live page: the Twitch channel, the YouTube live video, or the TikTok LIVE.
    - To keep the E6 visit counts, links go through the relay's own redirect, `https://livechat.deliciouswines.org/go/<id>`. It counts one visit and returns a 302 to the platform URL, with no interstitial page. This replaces the third-party link shortener, so the destination is always the named platform's own page.
    - The footer adds `Live data from Twitch, YouTube and TikTok · not affiliated`.
  - **Live data is ephemeral:**
    - Platform data includes viewer counts, titles and live status.
    - It lives only in memory or in a `live_streams` table. Rows are overwritten each poll and deleted when a stream ends or is older than 24 hours.
    - That table is excluded from the E2 snapshot and nightly backups. Backups hold only listing records the streamer supplied: handles, Discord ID, in-game name, timezone, review status, opt-in and "I'd pay" flags.
    - DR7B's "last stream peak" is kept for at most 24 hours. After that, the listing counts as having no stream recorded (0-2).
  - **Deletion on opt-out or removal:** within the DR2E immediate edit, delete the listing's platform data at once. Delete the listing record itself within 7 days, unless the streamer opts back in.
  - **Freshness labels stay per source:** DR2A/DR2B already handle this. The evidence time is the source poll time, not the message edit time.
  - **Public pages for the YouTube quota audit (card t_90e00261):**
    - Add `/terms` to the relay.
    - Extend `/privacy` to cover the network. It must say what is collected, the 24-hour live-data limit, the 7-day deletion and how to opt out, and link Google's privacy policy and the YouTube Terms of Service.
    - Both pages use the existing relay page layout (DESIGN.md).
  - **Open, not built:** Twitch Developer Agreement VI.C restricts sharing API data with third parties without written permission. The launch accepts this risk (Brendan, option 1). There is no outreach to Twitch unless he asks.
